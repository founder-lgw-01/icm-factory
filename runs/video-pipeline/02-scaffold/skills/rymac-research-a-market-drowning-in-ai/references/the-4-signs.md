# The 4 Signs You're Drowning in AI

The measuring stick for this market. Every figure a report produces gets tied
back to one of these 4 signs, the same way a trade report ties everything back
to how fast they answer the phone.

The wording below is the wording that ships. Do not reword it, do not make it
cleverer, and do not turn it into a technical checklist. A business owner has
to hear one of these and say "that's me."

## The 4 Signs

### Sign 1. You're drowning

Someone got handed AI as a second job. Or the owner did it to himself, at
night, after the real work was done.

Nobody owns it, so nobody gets good at it. The person doing it was hired to do
something else, and that something else is now late.

**Detect it by asking:** who handles AI here, and is that actually their job?

**Score it by:** the share of businesses where AI belongs to nobody, or belongs
to someone whose title has nothing to do with it.

### Sign 2. You're paying with nothing to show

The bill shows up every month. They cannot point at what it bought.

The spend is real, the output is a feeling. And the bill is small enough that
cancelling it never quite becomes this week's problem.

**Detect it by asking:** what did you spend on AI last month, and what did it
produce that you can point at?

**Score it by:** typical monthly AI spend for a business this size, next to the
share who can name a result.

### Sign 3. You're lost in the build

They built or bought something complicated. Files and folders would have done
it.

This is where almost everyone enters. It does not feel like a problem while it
is happening. It feels like progress.

**Detect it by asking:** how many tools, assistants, or automations do you own,
and how many did you use this week?

**Score it by:** tools owned against tools actually used. The gap is the sign.

### Sign 4. You can't get out

It is load-bearing now. Something real runs on it, so shutting it off Monday
costs more than paying for it.

This is the one that makes people buy instead of limping along for another
year.

**Detect it by asking:** what breaks if you turn it all off Monday morning?

**Score it by:** the share who describe themselves as stuck, locked in, or too
far in to start over.

## The ladder (the part that sets the price)

**These are not a checklist. They are a progression.** This is the most useful
thing in the file and it decides what to sell and for how much.

People enter at sign 3, drift into 2, land on 1, and end at 4.

| Rung | What they carry | How it feels to them | What to sell | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Early | 3 only | Exciting. Building. | Hardest sale. Nothing hurts yet. | No pain, no budget |
| Middle | 3 and 1 | Tiring. "It's a mess." | The build, at the low end of the band | Pain is real, damage is not |
| Late | 3, 1 and 2 | Expensive and embarrassing | The build at full price plus the monthly | Now it has a dollar figure |
| Trapped | All 4 | Stuck. Resigned. | Top of the band, and they are relieved | Cost of doing nothing is highest |

**Two rules that fall out of this:**

1. **Rung decides price, not company size.** A trapped 5-person shop pays more
   readily than an excited 50-person one.
2. **Never sell the trapped rung to an early one.** Someone at sign 3 is having
   fun. Telling them they are trapped calls them stupid, and the call ends.

Score every segment by which rung most of it sits on. A report that says "this
market carries all 4 signs" without saying where the weight sits has not
answered the pricing question.

## What it is costing them

Two leaks, always both. The second one is bigger and almost nobody quotes it.

```
The human leak
  wasted hours per month = hours a week on AI x 4.33
  loaded hourly rate     = salary / 2080 x 1.25
  human leak             = wasted hours x loaded hourly rate

The spend leak
  spend leak = monthly AI spend x share they cannot attribute to a result

Total monthly leak = human leak + spend leak
```

**Rules for using this model:**

- The 1.25 burden multiplier covers payroll tax, insurance, and overhead on a
  salaried person. It is the standard employer loading, not a made-up number,
  but label it anyway.
- Hours a week, the salary, the spend, and the attribution share ALL come from
  the research or from what the prospect said. Every one that had to be
  modeled gets marked MODELED with the arithmetic shown.
- Recovery is never 100%. An honest report states a recovery share, marks it
  MODELED, and shows the low end alongside it. Claiming the whole leak back is
  how a number gets disputed on a call.
- Give the answer in hours and dollars, never as a percentage.

## The price anchor

The anchor is not a margin percentage. It is **the salary they would otherwise
pay.**

Their real alternative is hiring somebody to handle AI. That is a person, at a
real wage, with payroll tax and insurance on top, who needs a desk and a
manager and 90 days to become useful.

Put the offer next to that number and the offer looks small. Put it next to
their AI subscription and it looks big. Always anchor against the hire.

The research has to return the going rate for whoever they would hire, in the
segment being scored, so the comparison is theirs and not borrowed.

## Mapping a complaint to a sign

The market never names the sign. They describe a symptom. Translating it is the
job, and an unmapped complaint is unfinished work.

| What they say | Sign | What it really means |
|---|---|---|
| "It's been a mess, we're just juggling things" | 1 | Nobody owns it, so it lands on whoever is closest |
| "I'm doing this at night after everything else" | 1 | The owner took the second job himself |
| "I don't know if it's even worth what we pay" | 2 | Spend with no output attached |
| "We have like 6 subscriptions I think" | 2 | Cannot name what is being paid for |
| "I built a handful of agentic workflows" | 3 | Building past the point of usefulness |
| "I know it's pretty ambitious" | 3 | Already suspects it, cannot stop |
| "It works great one day and terrible the next" | 3 | Complexity, not capability |
| "We're too far in to start over" | 4 | The trap, stated plainly |
| "Everything runs on it now" | 4 | Load-bearing, so the price of leaving is high |

Extend this table for whatever segment gets researched. Verbatim in the left
column, always.

## Worked example: the portfolio owner

A composite of a real conversation, anonymized. Treat it as the reference
specimen.

**The business.** Several locations across 2 industries, a construction arm,
a rental portfolio, 3 people involved. A 10 year sales background, no
technical background.

**What he built.** A diagram with 6 named assistants, a router that picks
between them, a librarian that is the only one allowed to write, a dedicated
server, a separate personal machine, and a web app to look at it all. The goal
behind every bit of it: know his numbers.

**What the conversation sounded like, in pattern:**

- It has been a mess, historically, just juggling things
- He has already created a handful of agentic workflows
- He knows it is pretty ambitious
- And the line that matters: they really don't need to be complex

**His score: signs 1 and 3. Not yet 2 or 4.**

He is drowning and he is lost in the build. He has not deployed, so he is not
paying much and nothing is load-bearing. **He is on the middle rung, pre-trap.**

**Why this matters more than any other line in this file:** he diagnoses
himself in the same conversation. He already knows it does not need to be
complex. He still cannot stop. That is the market in one person, and it proves
the sale is not about teaching them what is wrong. They know. The sale is
about handing them the simpler thing.

**Handling rule:** when you meet your own version of this person, use the
pattern freely. Nothing identifying about a real person goes into anything
public without their say-so.
