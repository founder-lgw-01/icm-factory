# Term Sheet

Every term used in the video, in plain language. If somebody uses one of these on
you and it does not match what is below, ask them to explain it again.

**From:** Stop Being the Errand Boy Between AI Agents
**Watch it:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=En4IeyJ8JoI

---

## The words people throw around

**Agent.** An AI set up to do a job on its own. That is it. The word sounds like a
team member, which is exactly why it sells so well.

**Multi agent.** Several of those, each owning a piece of a job and handing work to
each other. On a slide it looks like a team. In a small business it usually looks
like you retyping context.

**Single agent.** 1 of them doing the whole job. Boring, cheap, and usually the
right answer under about 10 people.

**Orchestration.** A fancy word for "something has to decide who does what next."
In most small setups that something is you, unpaid.

**Context.** Everything the AI knows in the moment you ask it something. Here is
the part that costs people money: it starts empty every single time unless you give
it somewhere to read from.

**Context window.** How much it can hold at once. Think of it as desk space, not
memory. When the desk is full, older things fall off.

**Handoff.** One agent passing work to the next. Every handoff is a place
information gets dropped, and nobody counts them when they sell you the setup.

## The words from this system

**ICM, Interpretable Context Methodology.** Jake Van Clief's method. The plain
version: your folder structure IS the system. Organize the files well enough and 1
worker can find what it needs without being told.

**The map.** One page that says what lives where. Not a manual. An address list. If
it needs scrolling, it stopped being a map.

**The filing cabinet.** Plain folders with plain text files. If you cannot open it
and read it without paying somebody, you do not own it.

**Fred.** The name I give the 1 worker. You cannot picture an "agent." Everybody
can picture a guy at a desk, and once you can picture him you can tell when the
setup is wrong.

**The put-back.** Closing the folder and returning it to the drawer when you are
done. The step everybody skips, and the reason desks turn into piles.

**Rot.** When what is written down quietly stops being true. Nothing breaks and
nothing turns red. It just keeps answering with old information.

**Drift.** Rot in slow motion. Files gradually disagreeing with each other and with
reality.

## The words about the work

**Text file.** A file with nothing in it but words. No formatting, no program
needed. The most boring and most durable thing you can own.

**Folder.** Exactly what you think. That is the point. The whole idea is that
nothing here is exotic.

**Claude Code.** The tool I use to sit inside a folder and work on it. Any capable
AI works. The folders are what matter, not the brand.

**Prompt.** What you type. Ranked third in importance behind your folders and the
model, which is the opposite of what most people are taught.

## The claims, with their honest limits

**"70% worse."** From a study of 180 setups: at its worst, using multiple agents
came out 70% worse than 1.

**"81% better."** The same study, at its best. Say both numbers or say neither.

**"Almost nobody your size."** The survivable version of the argument. Multi agent
works on a specific kind of job. Most small businesses do not have that job. That
is a different sentence from "it never works," and the difference is your
credibility.
