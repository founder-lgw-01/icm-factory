# Sell The Idea The Day You Have It

## The 48 hours, in order

I was on camera building a chiropractor's AI receptionist. Mid-build, talking
over my own screen, I said this out loud:

> "All right, files and folders, people."

That was it. That was the whole idea arriving. I wanted to go buy the domain
right then and I could not, because I was 20 minutes into a video and you do
not walk out of a video.

Here is what actually happened after that:

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Day 0, mid-video | The line comes out of my mouth. I keep recording |
| Day 1 | I buy filesnfolders.com and build the page in under 3 hours |
| Day 1, 1 a.m. | The page goes live. Nobody has seen it |
| Day 2, 10 p.m. | 89 people have read it. 25 handed over an email address |
| Day 3 | 100 read it. 35 opted in |

3 hours of build. 21 hours of clock. 28%.

## The part nobody wants to hear

I did not validate it. I did not survey anybody. I did not build a waitlist,
run a poll, or ask the community if they wanted it.

I had an idea on a Tuesday and a live page on a Wednesday.

The reason that works is not that I am fast. It is that **the page IS the
validation.** 89 real strangers reading a real page and 25 of them handing
over a real email address tells you more in 21 hours than 3 weeks of asking
people what they think.

People lie in surveys. Nobody lies with their email address.

## Why most ideas die in the gap

The gap between "I have an idea" and "somebody can read it" is where almost
every idea dies. Not because the idea was bad. Because the gap has a hundred
places to stop:

1. I should research the market first
2. I need a logo
3. What if the name is taken
4. I should map out all the features
5. Let me look at what 5 competitors are doing
6. I will start Monday

Every one of those sounds responsible. Every one of those is the same thing
wearing a different coat: **you are avoiding the moment a stranger tells you
the truth.**

## What I actually did in those 3 hours

Not much, and that is the point.

- 1 page. Not a site. A page
- 1 idea on it, the thing I said out loud on camera
- 1 thing to do on the page, hand over an email address
- No nav, no about, no blog, no pricing, no chat widget
- Ugly on purpose, because pretty takes 3 days and ugly takes 3 hours

The page did not need to be good. It needed to **exist while I still cared
about it.**

## The rule

**Ship the page while the idea is still hot, because the idea gets colder
every hour and the page never gets warmer on its own.**

You are not trying to build the thing. You are trying to find out, this week,
whether anybody wants the thing. A page and an email box answers that. A
month of planning does not.

## Run this prompt

Paste this into your AI along with this file:

```
I have an idea I have not shipped yet. Here it is:

[DESCRIBE YOUR IDEA IN 3 SENTENCES, in your own words, the way you would
say it out loud to a friend. Do not clean it up]

Here is who I think it is for:

[WHO. Be specific. "Small business owners" is not specific]

Your job is to get me to a live page in under 3 hours, not to a business plan.

1. Write the ONE sentence that goes at the top of the page. It has to say
   what they get, not what it is. Give me 3 versions and mark which one you
   would ship.
2. Tell me the ONE thing the visitor does on this page. Just one. If you
   list more than one, pick and defend it.
3. List everything I want to build that I should NOT build this week, and
   next to each one, the reason it is a stall and not a step.
4. Write the full page copy. Short lines. No jargon. Somebody who has never
   heard of me should understand it in 10 seconds.
5. Tell me the smallest number of real visitors that would prove or kill
   this, and how many days it should take to get them.

Do not ask me clarifying questions first. Make your best assumptions, state
them at the top, and give me the page.
```

Then go buy the domain. Today, not Monday.
