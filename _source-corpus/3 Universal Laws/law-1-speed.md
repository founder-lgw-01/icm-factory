# Law 1: Speed (The 5-Minute Window)

Yo! It's RyMac. Law 1 is the wedge, and it's the easiest one to sell because the math is brutal and the fix is a no-brainer.

## The numbers

A lead is a fresh thing, and it rots fast.

- You are **100x** more likely to reach a lead in 5 minutes than in 30. (MIT, published in Harvard Business Review, across 15,000 leads.)
- **78%** of buyers go with whoever answers first.
- Wait 5 to 10 minutes and your odds of qualifying that lead fall about **400%**.
- The average business takes over **40 hours** to respond.

40 hours, on a 5-minute window. By the time they call back, the lead is stone cold and already bought from whoever answered first.

## The fix

Answer every new lead in under 5 minutes, day or night. And don't just answer, offer them the thing they already want:

> "Hey, it's RyMac from Allodra.ai. Saw you were interested in X. Figured a text was easier. Do A, B, or C times work to grab a call?"

A human can't hold that line every time, every night, every weekend. AI can. Plug that one leak and you just recovered customers the business already paid to reach. They'll send you a Christmas card for life.

Paste this into your AI with this file:

```
The business: [WHAT THEY SELL + WHO TO].
How leads come in today: [web form / phone / DM / Facebook lead ad / etc.].
How fast they answer now, honestly: [minutes / hours / next day].
Average value of one new customer: [$].

Using Law 1 in this file:

   and label it a model).
2. Write the instant first-message the AI should send a new lead in under 5
   minutes. It should confirm what they asked about and offer 2 to 3 specific
   times to talk. Keep it human, in this business's voice.
3. Write the 2 follow-up texts if they don't reply in 15 minutes and 1 hour.
4. Give me the plain-English spec for the system: trigger (new lead in),
   action (send message in under 5 min), handoff (book the call or alert the
   owner). No jargon.
```
