# Point It At The Business

Here's the part that decides whether you have a business or a hobby.

Going from Dr Chen's front desk to Ally, my software company's receptionist, I
changed 3 text files.

Not 3 systems. Not 3 tools. 3 text files.

## The 3 that change

**Who she is.** A short text file describing the person answering the phone.
Chen's is warm and empathetic, because people calling a chiropractor are in
pain. Ally's is direct, plainspoken, a little irreverent, short sentences,
never hype.

**Her page.** What the business actually is, in her words. What it does, who it
serves, what happens after the call.

**Her prompt.** The instructions she runs on. Same shape both times. Different
contents.

That's the swap. Everything else in those 6 folders is identical.

## What doesn't change, and this is the surprising part

Same voice. Literally the same voice, out of the same voice engine, picked once.

When I build a new one I tell Claude I want the same voice, and it copies the
voice ID over. I don't go back and re-pick anything.

Same phone setup. Same calendar wiring. Same grading process.

Two employees who sound like the same human being and behave like opposites,
because the manner is in the text file, not in the voice.

## Why this is the leverage

An agency owner reading this has 6 client brands and rebuilds the thinking every
time.

Not because you're disorganized. Because the last build lives in a conversation
you had 3 weeks ago and you can't copy a conversation.

When the thinking lives in text files inside numbered folders, client 2 is a
copy and 3 edits.

## The mistake I'd make if I were you

You'll want to change more than 3 files.

You'll look at the roofer and think their situation is different enough that the
booking rules need rewriting. Sometimes they do. Most of the time you're
rewriting because it feels like real work, and editing 3 text files doesn't.

Change the 3. Run a call. Let the call tell you if anything else is wrong.

You may hear this called configuration over customization. It means you edit
what's different and you leave alone what isn't.

## The test that proves you did it right

Copy the folder. Change the 3 files. Then have your AI read both folders side by
side and tell you, folder by folder, what is identical and what is different.

If the answer is "6 folders identical, 3 text files different," you have a core.

If it comes back with differences scattered across all 6, you didn't copy a
system. You started over and gave it the same name.

I ran exactly that comparison on camera. The report it produced is linked in the
video description.

## Run this prompt

```
I have an AI setup that works for one client, and I want to point it at a second
client without rebuilding it.

Here is client 1 and what I built for them:
[describe the business, what the AI does for them, and where the instructions live]

Here is client 2:
[describe the new business and what they need]

Do this for me:

1. Tell me the SHORTEST list of text files I would have to change to serve
   client 2. Give me the actual file names and what goes inside each one.
2. For every other part of the setup, confirm out loud that it stays untouched.
   If something has to change that I did not expect, say which one and why.
3. Write me the first draft of each changed file, using client 2's real details.
4. Then tell me the ONE test I should run to find out whether the swap worked,
   before I put it in front of the client.

If my list of changed files is longer than 5, tell me which ones I am changing
out of habit rather than necessity.
```
