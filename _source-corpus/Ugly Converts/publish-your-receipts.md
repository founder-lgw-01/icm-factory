# Publish Your Receipts

## What I did at 19:53 in the video

I opened a page with my own numbers on it and read them out loud while they
were still wet.

Not a case study written 6 months later. Not "results may vary." The actual
count, from the actual page, at 1 in the morning, with the hour-by-hour bars
still moving.

- 100 people read the letter
- 35 handed over an email address
- 42 of the 111 who hit the site
- 24 hours from launch
- 0 dollars spent on traffic

I also said out loud that the title of the video says 28% and the number on
screen says 35%, because the title froze when I recorded the hook and the
number kept climbing.

## Why I say the unflattering part too

Because the unflattering part is what makes the rest believable.

Everybody online has a screenshot. Screenshots are free and screenshots lie
constantly. What almost nobody does is show the number that argues with their
own headline.

The second I say "the title says 28 and it is actually 35, here is why," the
whole page becomes credible. Not because 35 is better than 28. Because I just
proved I am reading the screen instead of performing it.

**Correcting your own claim in public is the cheapest trust you will ever
buy.**

## The 4 things I never put on screen

Receipts are not an excuse to be reckless. In this build there were 4 things
that never went on camera:

1. The contact list. Those are 35 real people's email addresses
2. A button-press counter that reads 56, because it counts double clicks and
   retries. It would have made my number look BETTER and it is not true
3. A visitor count that reads high for the same reason
4. Any readable email address anywhere in 30 minutes of screen recording

Number 2 matters most. **The rule is not "show good numbers." The rule is
"show true numbers," and sometimes true is lower than the flattering one
sitting right next to it.**

If you would not defend the number in a room with the person who paid you,
it does not go on screen.

## How to build a receipts page

You do not need software for this. You need one page that shows:

- **The headline number**, big, with what it actually counts underneath it in
  plain words
- **The denominator.** A conversion rate without the visitor count is not a
  number, it is a mood
- **The clock.** Over what period. 35% in a day and 35% in a year are
  different businesses
- **What you removed and why.** Say out loud which records you took out. I
  removed exactly 1: my own opt-in, the first record, 1 second after launch
- **The shape over time**, not just the total. An hour-by-hour bar tells the
  reader whether it is real or 1 lucky spike

Then link it. In the video description, in the letter, in the follow-up email.
A receipt you keep in a folder is not a receipt.

## The uncomfortable part

Publishing receipts means publishing them when they are bad too.

If you only show numbers when they are good, your audience learns that your
silence means it went badly, and now your silence is a signal. You did not
avoid the bad number. You just made it louder.

The people who do this well post the flat month. It costs almost nothing and
it is the entire reason anybody believes the good month.

## Run this prompt

Paste this into your AI along with this file:

```
I want to build a receipts page for something I have already done, so I can
stop making claims and start showing numbers.

Here is the thing and the raw numbers I have:

[WHAT IT WAS. Then every number you actually have: visitors, leads, sales,
revenue, time period, spend. Messy is fine, paste it raw]

Here is what I am NOT allowed to show:

[NAMES, EMAILS, CLIENT INFO, ANYTHING PRIVATE]

Build me the receipts page:

1. Pick the ONE headline number. Tell me why that one and not the others.
2. Under it, write what it actually counts, in plain words a stranger would
   understand. No marketing language.
3. Show me the denominator and the time window for every number.
4. Go find the numbers in what I gave you that FLATTER me but are not
   clean, and tell me to remove them. Explain what makes each one dirty.
5. Tell me which numbers I am missing that a skeptical reader would ask for
   first.
6. Write the "what I removed and why" section in my voice, honestly.
7. Write the full page copy, plain and short.

Be hard on me here. Assume the reader thinks I am exaggerating, because
most people online are.
```
