# Your Second Client By Tonight

A chiropractor paid me $4,000 for one of these.

The build was 30 minutes, because I'd done it once already and it was a copy.

I'm not telling you that to brag about the number. I'm telling you because the
gap between the price and the build time is the entire business, and it only
exists on the second one.

The first one cost me weeks. The first one always does. That's the one where you
figure out which 6 folders you need and what order they go in.

## What you're actually charging for

Not the 30 minutes.

The business owner is paying for the calls they stop missing. Somebody rings at
7pm with a problem and a credit card, and right now that call goes to voicemail
and then to a competitor.

That's the thing being fixed. Speed to the lead, after hours, and the times
they're already on the phone with somebody else.

An AI receptionist doesn't need to be better than their best day. It needs to be
better than nobody answering.

## Why an agency is sitting on this and not doing it

You already have the clients.

You already have their trust, their calendar, their intake process, and their
phone number. The hard part of selling this, which is being somebody they
believe, is already done.

What's been stopping you is that every AI build you've done felt like a
one-off. Which it was. Because it lived in a chat window and a tool, and neither
of those things copies.

## The move

Copy the folder. Change 3 of the files. You have a second client by tonight.

That sentence sounds like a slogan and it's the actual mechanic. I said it at the
end of the video with the folders still open on screen.

Copy the folder means the 6 numbered folders, copied intact, no thinking.

Change 3 files means who she is, her page, her prompt.

By tonight means the build. Not the sale.

## The order, because people get this backwards

Sell it first. Build it second.

You do not go build 6 receptionists on spec and then go looking for somebody who
wants one. You take the one you already built, show it to a client you already
have, and get a yes before you touch a folder.

I called mine on camera so people could hear her work. That's the demo. You have
one too, the moment you build one.

The chiropractor heard it before he paid.

## What to charge

I'm not putting a number on yours.

What I'll tell you is the math you need: know what it costs you to run, which for
mine is around 8 cents a minute, and know what one recovered call is worth to
that specific business.

A chiropractor's new patient and a roofer's new roof are not the same number, and
the price follows the second number, not the first.

If you can't say out loud what a missed call costs them, you're not ready to
quote them. Go find that out first. It's also the best sales question in this
entire business.

## Run this prompt

```
I run an agency and I already have clients. I want to work out which of them I
could put an AI receptionist in front of, and what to say to them.

Here are my current clients:
[list them: the business type, roughly how they get leads, and anything you know
about how they handle their phone]

Here is what I have already built or could build:
[describe it, even if it is rough]

Do this for me:

1. Rank my clients by who loses the most money to a missed call. Explain the
   ranking in one line each, using how their business actually gets paid.
2. For the top 3, tell me what a single missed call is likely worth to them, and
   tell me exactly what I would need to ask them to confirm that number. Do not
   invent the number, give me the question.
3. For the number 1 client, write me the message I send them. Short, in my own
   voice, no pitch language, ending in a question they can answer with one word.
4. Tell me what I should have ready to show them BEFORE I send it, so a yes does
   not leave me scrambling.
5. Tell me the 3 text files I would change to point my existing build at that
   specific client.

If none of my clients are a good fit, say that plainly and tell me what kind of
business I should be looking at instead.
```
