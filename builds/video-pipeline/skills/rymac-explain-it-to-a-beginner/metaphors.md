# The locked picture bank

Every picture in here was said on camera and proven on real buyers. The wording is locked.

**The rule: never invent a new one.** If the thing being explained has no picture
in this file, say so and ask him. A metaphor he did not say is a brand leak, and
it will not survive him reading it out loud.

**Where the deep bank lives**, so nothing gets rewritten that already exists:

- The desk and the card are the anchor pictures for every explanation.
- `community/skool/packages/errand-boy/` holds the honest limit.
- `community/skool/packages/folder-system-41-days/` holds the walk test.
- The live sales letter uses them the same way.
- The `term-sheet.md` in each package is the plain language dictionary.

---

## 1. The desk

**The picture.** Your AI has a desk. The desk has a size. When you set a new thing
down on a full desk, something already there slides off the edge onto the floor.

**What it is.** The context window.

**His words.**
> "There's a desk. It has a size. And when it fills up, things fall off the edge."

**The part that does the work.** When something falls off, it does not tell you.
It just answers anyway, and it sounds every bit as confident as when it is right.

> "That's your Tuesday. Same prompt, worse answer. Nothing was wrong with you.
> Something fell off the desk."

**Use it for:** why the AI felt sharp on Monday and vague on Thursday. Why a
longer, cleverer prompt is not the fix.

---

## 2. The card taped to the desk

**The picture.** There is 1 card and it is taped to the desk. It is always
there, so you never have to go and get it. All it says is where everything
lives. Where is the brand voice? Third drawer. Where is
the pricing? Second drawer.

**What it is.** A file called `CLAUDE.md`.

**His words.**
> "It isn't the work. It isn't your documents. It's a map."

**The part that does the work.** It is small on purpose. It costs almost nothing to
read, and it gets read every single time before anything else.

**Use it for:** why they never edit `CLAUDE.md`. Why it is 11 lines and that is
correct.

---

## 3. The drawer, and the numbers on it

**The picture.** It walks over and pulls out the 1 folder it needs. It sets that
folder on the desk and uses it. Then it puts the folder back. The desk never
fills up, because it was never holding everything. It was holding a map and 1
folder at a time.

The drawers are numbered because it can only hold 1 at a time, so they have to go
in in order. Research before you write. Write before you edit.

**What it is.** Folders, and folders named `01_`, `02_`, `03_` for the
stages that happen in order.

**His words.**
> "And yeah, the folders spell the website. That was not an accident."

**Use it for:** why folder names have numbers on the front. Why renaming a folder
reorders the work.

---

## 4. Looking in the drawer before it goes back

**The picture.** Before that folder goes back in the drawer, he looks at it. Not
because the AI is stupid. Because it is his drawer and he is the one who knows if
it is right.

**What it is.** The human review gate.

**His words.**
> "Every single step leaves a file sitting on your desk that you can open, read,
> and fix before anything moves forward. You never give up the wheel. You're just
> not typing as much."

**Use it for:** the person who is nervous about handing real work to AI. This is
the line that relaxes them.

---

## 5. The messenger boy between his own robots

**The picture.** He had built himself little departments. 1 AI was doing copy. 1
was writing code. 1 was doing research. All of them were good at their jobs.
He walked between them all day. He carried what this one said over to that one.
He explained the whole situation from scratch every time. Then he carried the
answer back.

> "I had automated the work and made myself the intern."

**What it is.** A multi agent setup. 6 desks, 6 piles, and not 1 of them can
see anybody else's.

**The part that does the work.** Nobody sold him a multi agent framework. He built
one by accident, 1 AI at a time, because each one on its own made sense.

> "The AI was not broken. My structure was."

**Use it for:** the villain. This is why the demo looks unbelievable and falls
apart the second you point it at real work. Aim it at the $5,000 course and the
swarm diagram, never at a person.

---

## 6. One fact, one home

**The picture.** His map file was 146 lines. 55 of them were a picture of his
folders, which felt organised and felt thorough. That exact same information was
written down in a second file too. He added a folder, updated 1 of them, and forgot
the other.

Now the AI is reading 2 files that disagree. It does not throw an error. It does
not ask which one is right. It picks one and answers with total confidence.

**His words.**
> "I could feel something was off for about a week and I couldn't name it. So the
> rule, tattoo it on your arm: one fact, one home. The second you write the same
> thing in 2 places, you've built yourself a slow leak."

**Use it for:** why a rule lives in exactly 1 file. Why splitting brands is about
separating the rules, not shuffling the files.

---

## 7. The share drive

**The picture.** If you have ever organised a share drive so your co-workers could
find things, you have already done the hard part. You just did it for people
instead of for a machine.

**Use it for:** the person who thinks this is technical. It is folders and a text
file. There is no complicated part, and people go hunting for one.

---

## 8. Monday morning

**The picture.** 10 minutes, costs nothing. Open the folder you already work in,
the real one where your stuff already lives. Make 1 file in it called `CLAUDE.md`.
Write 3 things: what this folder is for in 1 sentence, where things live as a short
list, and what you never want touched.

> "That's it. That's a context layer. You just did the thing everybody's making
> infographics about."

**The part that does the work.** You do not design a system on day 1. You fix
whatever annoyed you today and write down where you put it.

> "You keep re-explaining your brand voice? Make a file for the brand voice and put
> 1 line on the card saying where it is. Same move for pricing."

**Use it for:** the close on any teaching piece. Always end with the 10 minute
version they can do this afternoon.

---

## 9. Where he stands

Not a metaphor, a posture, and it is why the teaching lands.

> "I'm not ahead of you. I'm 50 days in. I've broken most of this at least once.
> And I'm still the slowest guy in that room. I'm just close enough to the start to
> still remember it."

He is not the expert on the mountain. He is the guy 1 mile up the road who still
remembers the first mile. Never write him as the authority correcting them. Write
him as the guy who bounced off every explanation of this he could find.

> "That is not on the people who wrote them. They're right and they're a lot
> smarter than me. They're just standing further down the road than I was."

**Never** let a teaching piece imply the reader is slow. The line is always:

> "You are not bad at this. I know it feels like you are."
