# Research Before You Write A Word

Copywriting is about 80% research. If the copy is hard to write, the research
is not done. You are not blocked, you are guessing.

On the build I sent 3 agents out at once to mine the market before I wrote a
single headline. Here is what came back, and the 2 things that changed the
page.

## What I had them mine

Not keywords. Not competitors' features. **The words real people use when
they are frustrated.**

Reddit threads, forum posts, reviews of the tools they already tried and
hated, the complaints under YouTube videos. The stuff people type at 2am when
nobody is selling them anything.

Then I had each one hand back verbatim quotes tagged by what they were:
pain, desire, objection, failed alternative, trigger.

Verbatim. Not summarized. The moment you paraphrase a customer you have
launched out the exact thing you went looking for.

## Finding 1: the market is burned out, not uninformed

The research came back showing this market is what copywriters call stage 5.
Not "they have never heard of this." Worse. **They have heard every claim
and stopped believing all of them.**

Real quotes that came back:

- "Most of the money to be made surrounding AI agents is by selling courses about how to make money using AI agents."
- "If such returns were possible, the course seller would just trade instead of selling courses."
- "Many consumers have a working bullshit detector."

Here is what that does to your copy. **At stage 5 you cannot lead with a
claim, because every claim is spent.** You lead with identification or with
radical honesty. You tell them something true about themselves that a liar
would not bother saying.

If I had skipped the research I would have written a bigger promise. That
would have been exactly wrong, and I would never have known why it did not
convert.

## Finding 2: the claim I was about to make was false

I was going to say nobody teaches the selling half.

The research found a direct competitor selling exactly that, at $77 a month,
with 391 members. My claim was not edgy, it was just wrong, and 1 person in
the comments would have known it.

**That is worth more than any headline the research produced.** It killed a
lie before it went on a live page.

What survived is better anyway: not that nobody teaches it, but WHO is
teaching it. Their guy is a builder who added sales. I am a closer who
learned to build. That is not a claim anyone can copy, because it is a
biography.

## The bit most people skip

The research also handed back the words they use and the words they never
use. In my market they say crickets, shipped, traction, $0 MRR. They never
say pipeline, prospecting, discovery call, close rate.

So a closer's page had to be written without a single closer's word.

If I write "book a discovery call" to that audience, I have announced I am
from the other tribe, and it does not matter how good the offer is.

## Run this prompt

```
I sell [YOUR OFFER] to [YOUR BUYER]. Before I write any copy, mine the
market for me.

Search Reddit, forums, and reviews of the tools or services my buyer has
already tried. I want how they actually talk, not a summary.

Hand back:

1. 20 to 30 VERBATIM quotes. Never paraphrase. Tag each one: pain, desire,
   objection, failed alternative, or trigger. Include where it came from.
2. The 1 dominant pain, in their exact words, ranked by how often it shows
   up times how angry it sounds.
3. The trigger moment. What happens right before they go looking for a fix?
4. Their vocabulary: 10 to 15 words they DO use, and the words they never
   use that people in my industry say all the time.
5. The sophistication stage, 1 to 5. Stage 1 means they have never heard the
   claim. Stage 5 means they have heard everything and believe none of it.
   Tell me which, with the quotes that prove it.
6. The claims so overused in my market that they now read as lies. I want a
   banned list.
7. Anything I currently believe about this market that the research says is
   FALSE. Be blunt. I would rather find out here than on a live page.

If you cannot find a real quote for something, say so. Do not invent one.
```

Point 7 is the one that earns its keep. Run it before you write, not after
the page underperforms.
