# The Desk

Yo! It's RyMac. This file is the core mechanic from the video. If you only
read one of these, read this one, because every other file assumes it.

## The thing nobody tells you

When you ask an AI something, it does not magically know anything about you,
or your company, or your files. It reads. It pulls things in front of it and
works off what it can see right then.

Picture a desk.

Your question lands on it. Then it starts stacking: your document, that other
document, the thread from yesterday, the 4 files it figured might be relevant.
It reads, and it reads, and it puts everything it thinks it needs on that desk.

**The desk has a size.** It is not infinite. When it fills up, things fall off
the edge.

And here is the part that actually costs you money: **when something falls off,
it does not tell you.** It does not say "hey, I lost your brand guide." It just
quietly answers you without it, with total confidence.

That is your Tuesday. Same prompt as Monday, worse answer. Nothing was wrong
with you. Something fell off the desk.

## The real name

The desk is called the **context window**. The stuff on it is called
**context**. That is the whole vocabulary.

So the next time you are reading a thread and somebody drops "context
engineering" on you like it is a 6 syllable way to make you feel dumb, they
are talking about what goes on the desk and what does not. That is it.

## Why this reframes everything

Most people, when the output gets worse, conclude they are bad at prompting.
They go learn a better prompt. They buy a prompt pack.

The prompt was never the problem. **You were handing it a pile and hoping the
right things stayed on the desk.**

Once you see the desk, the fix is obvious and it is not a prompt. It is:
stop putting the pile on the desk. Put a map on the desk instead, and let it
go get one thing at a time. That is the next file.

## Run this prompt

Paste this into your AI along with this file:

```
I want to find out what is falling off the desk in my own AI work.

Here is a task I ask an AI to do regularly, and the stuff it needs to know
to do it well:

[DESCRIBE THE TASK]
[LIST EVERYTHING IT NEEDS TO KNOW: brand voice, pricing, customer types,
past examples, rules, tone, anything]

Do 4 things using the desk idea from this file:
1. Estimate roughly how much of that list can realistically sit in front of
   you at once, and say which items are most likely to get dropped first.
2. Point at the specific items where, if they silently went missing, I would
   NOT notice the answer got worse. Those are the dangerous ones.
3. Tell me which of these things need to be loaded EVERY time versus only
   when I am doing this one task.
4. Give me a one-sentence description of what this task's folder is for,
   written so a stranger would understand it.

Be blunt about what I am overloading.
```

Hold onto the answer to number 3 and number 4. You need both for the next file.
