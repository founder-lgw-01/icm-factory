# Term Sheet

Every word used in the video, in one line each.

If somebody uses one of these on you and their explanation doesn't match what's
here, ask them to explain it again.

---

**AI receptionist** · a voice that answers the phone for a business, asks the
questions a good front desk asks, and books the appointment on the real calendar.

**The core** · the part of a build you don't do twice. For me it's 6 numbered
folders that are identical across every client.

**Folder system** · your business written into text files inside folders, so your
AI can read it instead of you re-explaining it every time.

**ICM** · Interpretable Context Methodology. Jake Van Clief's method. The folder
system is how you run it.

**CLAUDE.md** · the card taped to the desk. The one file that tells your AI where
everything else lives.

**Context** · what your AI can see right now. Not what you told it last week.
Just what's on the desk in front of it.

**Context window** · how much fits on that desk at once. When it's full, the
oldest things fall off, and it doesn't tell you they fell.

**Voice engine** · the software that turns text into the sound of a person. I use
ElevenLabs. You pick a voice once and copy the ID after that.

**Voice ID** · a short code for the exact voice you picked. Copying it is how a
second receptionist ends up sounding like the first one.

**Vapi** · the software that puts the voice on a phone number. Handles the
calling part. You can get free numbers from it if you're not sending texts.

**A2P** · the approval process for sending business text messages. Only matters
if you're texting, not if you're only taking calls.

**The prompt** · the instruction file the receptionist runs on. Same prompt drives
the phone and the chat box on the website.

**Grade sheet** · the list of things a great call does for that business, used to
mark a real transcript done or not done.

**Test call** · you calling your own receptionist to find out where it falls down,
before a customer does.

**Retest** · calling again after a fix, to confirm the fix landed. Skipping this
is how a "fixed" receptionist ships broken.

**Speed to lead** · how fast somebody who raised their hand gets a response. Most
missed money in a service business is here.

**After hours** · the calls that come in when nobody's at the desk. The reason the
thing pays for itself.

**Screening** · asking questions to find out whether a caller is a fit before
putting them on a calendar. Ally does this. Chen's front desk doesn't.

**Per minute cost** · what it costs to run a call. Mine sits around 8 cents,
somewhere in a 6 to 10 range depending on how complex the receptionist is.

**Sell first, build second** · get the yes before you build the thing. The
chiropractor heard the demo before he paid.

**Agent** · one worker with a map. Not 7 of them talking to each other.

**The errand boy** · you, carrying information between your own AI tools by hand,
because none of them can see the same desk.
