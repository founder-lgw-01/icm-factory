# Term Sheet

Every term of art from the video, in plain language. If a word in here still
does not make sense after 1 line, that is my fault, tell me in the community
and I will rewrite it.

## The page and the numbers

**Landing page** A single web page with one job. Somebody arrives, reads it,
and does one thing. It is not a website, it is one page.

**Opt-in** Somebody hands over their email address on purpose. The word just
means they chose to be on your list.

**Conversion rate** Out of everybody who showed up, the share who did the
thing you wanted. 89 people read the letter and 25 gave an email address,
which is 28%.

**Denominator** The bottom number in that math. The "out of how many." A
percentage without it is meaningless and usually hiding something.

**Referrer** Where a visitor came from. "No referrer" means the browser did
not say, which usually means they typed it in or clicked from an app.

**Sales letter** A page written like a letter to one person instead of a
brochure written to an audience. Old format, still the highest converting
thing I know of.

**Headline** The first line on the page. It has one job: earn the second line.

**CTA** Short for call to action. The one thing you are asking them to do.
The button and the line above it.

## The build

**Claude Code** A version of Claude that runs on your computer and can read
and write real files, not just chat. It is what built the page in the video.

**Domain** The address people type. filesnfolders.com is a domain. You rent it
yearly from a registrar, usually 10 to 20 dollars.

**Hosting** Where the page actually lives so the world can see it. I use
Vercel and for a page like this it is free.

**Deploy** Pushing your new version live so visitors see it. In the video it
takes about 15 seconds.

**Live** The version the public sees right now, as opposed to the one on your
computer.

**Repo** Short for repository. The folder holding your project plus its
history, so you can see what changed and go back.

**Push** Sending your changes up so hosting can pick them up and deploy.

**Skill** A saved set of instructions you can hand an AI so it does a job your
way instead of the generic way. Mine wrote the letter in my voice because my
approach is in a skill it can read.

**Artifact** A page an AI builds for you that you can open in a browser and
share with a link. The receipts page in the video is one.

## The folder system

**ICM folder system** Jake Van Clief's method: put the context next to the
work, in folders your AI can walk. Free at filesnfolders.com.

**Context** Everything the AI needs to know about your situation before it
does the job. Who you are, who you sell to, what you already decided.

**Context layer** A file inside a folder that says what this folder is for.
The AI reads it on the way in so it knows where it is.

**State file** The file that says what is true right now. Done, next, changed.
This is what stops you re-explaining your business every session.

**Hard rules** The short list of things that must never happen. Usually
written after one of them already happened and cost you.

**Agent** The word everybody uses for an AI doing a job on its own. I avoid it
because it makes 1 worker sound like a department.

**Multi-agent workflow** Several AI workers handing tasks to each other. It
looks great in a diagram and loses your context at every handoff.

## The traffic

**Traffic loop** The system that takes one video and turns it into every other
surface: the description, the community post, the blog post, the email. One
recording, many doors.

**Lead magnet** The free thing somebody gets for handing over an email
address.

**UTM tag** Extra text on the end of a link that tells you which video or post
sent that visitor. It is why the link in the description looks longer than it
needs to.

**End card** The clickable video suggestion at the end of a YouTube video.

**Chapters** The timestamps in a YouTube description that let people jump.
