# The Honest Limit

**From:** Stop Being the Errand Boy Between AI Agents
**Watch it:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=En4IeyJ8JoI

---

## The 30 second version

Multi agent is not a scam. It is just not for a business your size.

That version of the claim survives a smart person pushing back. The absolute
version does not, and if you go around saying "agents never work" you will get
taken apart in public by somebody with a link.

---

## The numbers, both directions

The study people quote tested 180 setups.

At its worst, multiple agents came out **70% worse**.

At its best, they came out **81% better**.

It also found many more errors when the agents did not coordinate, and a rough rule
fell out of it: **if 1 agent is already doing the job reasonably well, adding more
agents makes it worse.**

And the company that makes the model ships a multi agent system of their own.

So the honest read is not "it never works." It is: it works on a specific kind of
job, and almost nobody your size has that kind of job.

---

## Why this matters more than being right

You are going to sell this idea to somebody. A client, a partner, your own team.

If your pitch is "agents are fake," the first person who has actually made one work
kills your credibility in one sentence, in front of everybody, and you deserve it.

If your pitch is "that works at a scale neither of us is at, here is what works at
ours," you cannot be knocked over. You already said the strongest thing your
opponent had.

**Naming the limit of your own argument is not weakness. It is the thing that makes
the rest of it believable.**

---

## What the job needs to look like for many agents to win

Be able to say these out loud about a task before you split it up:

**1. It is genuinely big enough to split.** Not "it has steps." Steps are not
splits. Different jobs needing different knowledge is a split.

**2. The pieces can share what they learn.** If piece 2 cannot see what piece 1
figured out, you did not build a team, you built a relay where you are the baton.

**3. Somebody maintains it.** Not you, in the cracks of your week. It is a
maintained thing or it is a decaying thing.

**4. 1 worker already tried and genuinely failed.** Not "was slow." Failed in a way
a better organized set of files would not fix.

If you cannot say all 4, you are buying complexity and calling it capability.

---

## The villain is the pattern, never a person

There are people selling a 5,000 dollar course on building this.

Most of them do not even use AI the way you actually need to. They sell a course to
make money. I use 1 worker, folders, and text files to make money.

Notice I did not name anybody. **Do not name the guru.** The moment you do, a
teaching video becomes a fight, and you hand them your audience for free.

Attack the pattern. Leave the person alone. It is better business and it is better
manners.

## Run this prompt

This one pressure tests YOUR pitch before a prospect does it for you.

```
I am going to give you my argument for why a business like mine should use 1
organized AI worker instead of multiple AI agents. I want you to try to destroy it.

My argument as I would say it to a client:
[WRITE IT OUT THE WAY YOU ACTUALLY SAY IT]

Who I am saying it to: [DESCRIBE THE CLIENT AND THEIR SIZE]

Do this in 3 passes:

PASS 1, THE SKEPTIC. You are a sharp technical person who has personally built a
working multi agent setup. Push back hard on everything I said that is too
absolute. Quote my own words back at me. Find every place I overclaimed.

PASS 2, THE REPAIR. Rewrite my argument so it survives you. Rules for the rewrite:
- It must openly admit where multiple agents genuinely do win
- It must never say "never" or "always"
- It must not name or insult any specific person or company
- It must still land on a clear recommendation, not mush
- Keep my voice, do not make it corporate

PASS 3, THE TEST. Give me the 3 hardest questions a prospect could ask after
hearing the repaired version, and a short honest answer for each. If the honest
answer is "that is a fair point and here is the tradeoff," say that.

Write everything at a 9th grade reading level. No jargon.
```
