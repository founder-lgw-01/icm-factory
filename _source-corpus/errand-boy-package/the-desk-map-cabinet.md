# The Desk, The Map, and The Filing Cabinet

**From:** Stop Being the Errand Boy Between AI Agents
**Watch it:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=En4IeyJ8JoI

---

## The 30 second version

You do not need 7 agents. You need 1 worker who can find things.

A desk. A map taped to it. A filing cabinet. That is the entire system, and it is
deliberately boring.

---

## Meet Fred

I call the 1 worker Fred, because you cannot picture an "agent" but everybody can
picture a guy at a desk.

That is not a cute naming thing. It is the whole wedge. The moment you can picture
him, you can tell when the setup is wrong, because you can ask a normal human
question: **would a new guy be able to find this?**

Fred is not clever. Fred is fast because he has a map.

---

## The walkthrough, exactly as it runs

I walk up to Fred's desk and ask where we stand on a project.

He looks at the map I leave taped in front of him. The map points to 1 file that
gives him the background. That file tells him which drawer to look in. He goes,
opens 1 drawer, takes 1 folder, and brings it back.

"We stand here on this project. What do you wanna do next, boss?"

Then I tell him to put that folder back and go work on a different website. **The
folder goes back in the drawer.** He looks down at the map again. Different file,
different drawer, and he brings me that one.

He checks the map every single time.

That repetition is not a detail. **That repetition IS the mechanism.** It is why
nothing drifts and why a brand new session can pick up where you left off without
you explaining anything.

---

## The 3 pieces, in plain terms

**1. The desk.** Wherever the work happens. For me that is Claude Code sitting in a
folder on my computer. It has no memory of yesterday and that is fine, because of
piece 2.

**2. The map.** ONE page. It says what lives where and nothing else. Not a manual,
not a strategy doc. An address list. If your map needs scrolling, it is not a map
anymore.

**3. The filing cabinet.** Plain folders with plain text files. No database, no
platform, no subscription. Files you could open in Notepad and read yourself.

That last part is the ownership test: **if you cannot read it without paying
somebody, you do not own it.**

---

## The rules that keep it working

**1 thing lives in 1 place.** The second a price, a promise, or a policy exists in
2 files, they will disagree, and your AI will confidently pick the wrong one.

**Name folders for what they DO, not what they are.** "Write copy" beats
"Marketing." You want the map to read like directions, not like a filing system a
consultant sold you.

**Put the map back.** Every single time. The instinct is to keep the folder open
because you might need it again. That is exactly how the desk turns into a pile.

**Boring beats clever.** Every clever thing you add is a thing you have to remember
later, and you will not.

---

## What this replaces

It replaces the 7 agents talking to each other and never getting a folder to the
front.

1 organized worker using text files and folders runs circles around 7 agents on
bloated software, and the reason is not intelligence. It is that the organized one
never has to guess.

---

## Run this prompt

This builds YOUR map and YOUR cabinet from what you already have. It does not ask
you to buy anything.

```
I want to set up a simple folder system so that any AI session can figure out my
business without me re-explaining it every time.

My business: [DESCRIBE IT IN 3 SENTENCES]
What I actually sell: [LIST IT]
The kinds of work I do over and over: [LIST 4 TO 8 THINGS]
Where my important information currently lives: [BE HONEST, INCLUDING "IN MY HEAD"]

Do these 4 things, in order, and stop after each one so I can react:

1. Draft my MAP. One page, maximum. It only says what lives where, like an address
   list. No strategy, no explanation. Show it to me as plain text.

2. Design my FILING CABINET: the folder names, in the order the work actually
   happens. Name folders for what gets DONE in them, not for what they are. Keep
   it under 10 top-level folders. Tell me which ones I do not need yet.

3. For each folder, tell me the 1 file that has to exist inside it for the folder
   to be useful, and 1 sentence on what goes in that file.

4. Give me a "1 thing, 1 place" check: list anything from my description that is
   likely to end up written down in 2 different spots, and tell me which single
   spot it should live in.

Rules for your output:
- Plain English at a 9th grade level. Define any technical word in the same
  sentence.
- Everything is plain folders and plain text files. Do not suggest a tool, an app,
  or a subscription.
- If something I described is genuinely not worth organizing yet, say so and tell
  me to skip it.
```
