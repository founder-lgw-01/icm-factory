# Anytime you write for the owner, write in their voice and style

An AI has a house style. Long paragraphs, colons chaining clauses, tidy summary
sentences. Real people don't write that way, and a reader can feel the difference
in 2 lines.

This skill exists because AI-cadence prose once shipped inside paid course
materials. The buyer noticed before the owner did. Nothing published under the
owner's name gets written that way again.

## Calibrate first, write second

The owner's own writing is the spec. Before writing a word:

1. Open `_config/my-voice/` and read 1 of the samples in it, top to bottom.
2. If that folder is empty, STOP and ask the owner to paste 2 or 3 pieces they
   actually published. An email, a post, a video description. Save them there.
3. Never write from a guess about how they sound.

When the owner has written a swipe or an example for a format, the swipe is the
law and this file just points at it.

## What strong published writing usually looks like

- **Short lines. 1 thought per line.** Then a blank line. The next thought.
- A paragraph is 1 to 3 short sentences. Never a wall.
- Fragments are legal when the owner uses them.
- Repeat a word for punch instead of reaching for a synonym.
- Plain words a 9th grader knows. A technical term gets the sandwich
  ("you may hear that called X, it means Y") or gets cut.

## Banned constructions, the AI tells

These are the defaults. The owner can edit this list in `_config/my-line.md`,
and their own published writing always outranks it.

1. **The colon-clause chain.** "The build: the seat, the calendar, and the
   wiring." Break it into lines.
2. **The comma train.** 3 or more commas in a sentence is a rewrite.
3. **Em dashes.** The single most recognizable AI tell. Use the punctuation the
   sentence actually wanted.
4. **Spelled-out numbers.** Digits always.
5. **Adjective stacking.** "Complete, thorough, step-by-step guide" is filler.
   Pick 1 word or 0.
6. **Developer talk** on anything public. No pipeline, schema, deploy talk
   without the sandwich.
7. **The tidy summary sentence** that restates what was just said. End on the
   point, not on a bow.

## The procedure

1. Open 1 of the owner's reference pieces. Read 10 lines of it. THEN write.
2. Write the piece in their cadence.
3. Sweep before delivering: colons chaining clauses, comma count per sentence,
   em dashes, spelled numbers, dev talk.
4. If a sentence would not survive the owner reading it out loud on camera,
   rewrite it.

## When it fires

Every time text is written FOR the owner: community posts and lesson
descriptions, YouTube titles and descriptions, package and course files, drop
posts, emails, blog posts, DMs, scripts, page copy they will paste from. Named
or not. Other skills (`rymac-package-a-video-for-your-community`,
`rymac-write-an-seo-blog-post`, the copy skills) run WITH this one, and this one
wins on cadence.
