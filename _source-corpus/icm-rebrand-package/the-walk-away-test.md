# The Walk-Away Test

From the video: "1 Prompt Rebuilt My Entire Content System (Jake Van Clief ICM Folder System)"

## Jake's rule

"If you can't ask it to do something and walk away, you're doing it wrong."

That line is Jake Van Clief's, and it's the whole test. Not "can the AI do impressive things while you babysit it." Can you hand it a real job, leave the house, and trust what's waiting when you get back?

My test: 8 training videos wearing a dead brand. The old fix was a week of re-recording, re-editing, re-uploading. I typed 1 prompt describing the outcome I wanted, got in the truck, and went to Walmart for the pooper scooper I forgot the first trip. The errand WAS the walk-away window.

## What makes walking away safe

I didn't gamble. 3 things made the test safe:

1. **I described the outcome, not the steps.** "Rebrand all 8 videos, cut my old closes, new end card, and do not make me reshoot anything." The last part is a guardrail: it told the machine what it was NOT allowed to do.
2. **Nothing went live without me.** The machine built everything, then stopped and waited. I watched a 90 second preview and typed 1 word: approved. A walk-away test with no approval gate at the end isn't brave, it's careless.
3. **The work was checkable.** Every cut was verified word by word against what's actually in the finished files. 1 video tried to sneak the first word of an old ending past the cut, and the check caught it before I ever saw it.

## Start smaller than I did

My test was 8 videos because my folders had 41 days of structure under them. Your first test should be 1 thing you could redo by hand in under an hour if it flopped. The point of test 1 is not the output. It's finding out what your AI didn't know, so you can write it down where it can read it next time.

## Run this prompt

Paste this into Claude (or any AI):

```
I want to run my first walk-away test: give you a real job, leave, and judge what's waiting when I get back.

The job I'm considering: [describe 1 real task, like "rewrite my 5 service pages in my new tone" or "turn my last 3 videos into blog posts"]
What you already know about my business: [paste your brand rules, examples of your voice, or say "not much, and that's probably a problem"]

Before I leave, do this:
1. Tell me if this job is walk-away safe. If redoing it by hand would take me more than about an hour, suggest a smaller first test.
2. Ask me every question you'd otherwise have to guess at. My answers become part of the job.
3. Restate the job as an outcome with guardrails: what you will do, what you will NOT do, and where you will stop and wait for my approval before anything goes live.
4. Then do the job. When I get back, show me: what you produced, what you had to guess at anyway, and what I should write down so the next test needs fewer questions.
```
