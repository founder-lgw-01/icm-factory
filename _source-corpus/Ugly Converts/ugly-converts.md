# Ugly Converts

## The number first

89 visitors. 25 email addresses. **28%.**

By the time I recorded the video it had moved to 100 read and 35 opted in,
which is 35%.

For reference, a landing page that converts at 5% is considered fine. 10% is
considered good. This page is not pretty, has no video, no testimonials, no
logos, no countdown timer, and no design system.

It is a letter on a cream page.

## What is actually on the page

That is the whole thing. Somebody lands, reads a letter, and either hands over
an email address or leaves.

There is no nav bar, so there is nowhere to wander off to. There is no pricing
page, because nothing is for sale. There is no About page, because the letter
IS the about page.

**Every element I did not build is an exit I did not give them.**

## Why the letter format beats the pretty page

A pretty page says "a company made this."

A letter says "a person wrote this, to me."

The second one is worth more, and it is worth more precisely because it looks
like it took less effort. Polish reads as marketing. Marketing gets defenses
up. A plain letter in plain type reads as somebody talking, and people do not
defend against somebody talking.

I did not invent this. Direct mail figured it out 60 years ago and it did not
stop being true because we got CSS.

## The 4 things doing the work

Strip everything else away and this is what earned the 28%:

1. **1 reader.** The letter is written to one person, not to an audience. The
   word "you" does the heavy lifting. Never "our users," never "businesses."
2. **1 idea.** The page argues exactly one thing. If you cannot say what the
   page is about in one sentence, the page is about nothing.
3. **1 ask.** 1 box, 1 button. The moment there are 2 things to do, the
   most common choice becomes neither.
4. **A real story with real numbers.** Not "proven results." An actual thing
   that actually happened, with the actual figures, including the ones that
   are not flattering.

## The trap

The trap is thinking the design is what is broken.

When a page does not convert, almost nobody's first instinct is "the words are
wrong." They redesign it. New colors, new hero image, new font, a video at the
top. The page gets prettier and the number does not move, because the number
was never about how it looked.

**A page converts on what it says and who it says it to. The look only has to
stay out of the way.**

Ugly stays out of the way for free.

## What ugly does NOT mean

It does not mean sloppy. There is a difference:

| Ugly, which is fine | Sloppy, which is not |
|---|---|
| Plain type on a plain background | Text overlapping other text |
| No images | A broken image icon |
| One column, no layout tricks | Lines running off the side of a phone |
| Built in 3 hours | Typos in the headline |
| No brand colors | The email box does not actually work |

Ugly is a choice about where to spend the effort. Sloppy is not testing the
email box.

## Run this prompt

Paste this into your AI along with this file:

```
Here is my current landing page copy, exactly as it reads today:

[PASTE YOUR PAGE COPY. If you do not have a page yet, paste how you
describe your offer when somebody asks what you do]

Here is who it is for:

[WHO, specifically]

Judge it against the 4 things in this file. Be blunt with me, I would rather
be embarrassed now than watch it convert at 1%.

1. ONE READER: mark every line written to a crowd instead of to one person.
   Rewrite each one to a single reader.
2. ONE IDEA: tell me in one sentence what this page argues. If you cannot,
   say so and tell me which 3 ideas are fighting each other.
3. ONE ASK: list every single thing this page asks the visitor to do,
   including links, nav, and anything that lets them leave. Then tell me
   which one to keep and what to delete.
4. REAL PROOF: mark every claim that has no number or no specific story
   behind it. For each one, tell me exactly what receipt would fix it.

Then rewrite the whole page as a plain letter to one person. Short lines.
No headers that sound like a brochure. No feature lists. Assume they will
read the first 2 lines and decide.

Last, tell me the ONE thing on the page most likely to be costing me
conversions, and why.
```

Ship the ugly one. You can make it pretty after it works.
