# E1 Is ALWAYS "Here's Your Stuff"

You built the page. Somebody put their email in. Now what?

Most people get this backwards and it costs them the whole list.

## The rule

**Email 1 delivers. Always. No exceptions.**

Not "welcome to the community." Not "here's a bit about me." Not "let me tell
you why I built this." Deliver the thing they just asked for, in the first
email, before you ask for anything.

## Why I am rigid about it

On my page, submitting the form redirects them straight to the free course.
Instant. So you could argue email 1 does not need to deliver anything, they
already got it.

Wrong, and here is why.

The redirect can fail. They can close the tab. They can be on a phone at a
red light with no time to register. **The email is the only thing that
survives all 3.**

So E1 hands over the link again AND delivers something they can use without
registering anything at all. Both halves, because I do not know which one
reached them.

That is not belt and braces. That is the difference between a lead who got
what you promised and a lead who feels lied to on day 1.

## E2 is the formal introduction

Only after E1 has landed do you say who you are.

Short. One click to something about you. No teaching. My E2 is 6 sentences
and a link to a video, and it ends on the 1 line that separates me from
everyone else in my space.

Order matters: **keep the promise, then earn the right to be interesting.**
Do it the other way round and you are just another person in their inbox
talking about themselves.

## The 3 rules that keep the rest alive

**Never send a naked email.** Every single one has something to click. The
moment you send one with nothing in it, you have taught them some of your
emails are skippable, and they cannot tell in advance which ones. So they
skip the one that mattered.

**Never attach anything.** Attachments wreck deliverability at volume and
half the clients strip them anyway. Host it, link to it.

**Every P.S. promises the next one, specifically.** Not "more soon." Name the
thing. My E2 ends promising the number that changed how I run every business,
and E3 opens with "told you I would send this." That loop is checkable. If
they remember the promise and it shows up, you have trained them that your
emails pay off.

## Cadence

Tight at the front while attention is hot, widening as it goes: day 0, 1, 3,
4, 6, 9, 11, 14, 18, 21. Then every 2 to 4 days after that.

Notice it is jittered. Not every 3 days like clockwork. **A perfectly regular
cadence reads like a machine, and an irregular one reads like a person who
had something to say.**

## The part that pays you in 6 months

I loaded 22 emails, not 10. That was deliberate.

Somebody who goes quiet at email 6 is not gone, they are dormant. When you
ship something new and it lands in their inbox 3 months later, that is the
cheapest reactivation there is. You never lost them, they just stopped
needing you for a while.

One rule when you add to it: **append to the end, never insert in the
middle,** and rewrite the previous last email's P.S. so it promises the new
one. Insert into the middle and every P.S. after that point promises the
wrong thing.

## Run this prompt

```
Write the first 3 emails of my follow-up sequence.

What people opted in to get: [THE THING YOU PROMISED ON THE PAGE]
Where that thing lives: [LINK OR "not hosted yet"]
Who I am, in 3 lines: [THE SHORT VERSION. What makes you the person to
listen to on this?]
Something about me they can click: [VIDEO, PAGE, POST. If none, say so.]

Rules, follow all of them:

1. Email 1 delivers the thing. Nothing else. Assume the download or redirect
   they just went through may have failed, so hand it over again here.
2. Email 2 is my formal introduction. Short. No teaching. Exactly 1 click.
3. Email 3 teaches 1 useful thing and gives it away free.
4. Every email has something to click. No exceptions.
5. Nothing is attached. Everything is a link.
6. Every P.S. names specifically what the next email contains, and the next
   email opens by paying that promise off.
7. Plain English, short sentences, no hype, no income claims.

Then give me the send days, tight at the front and widening, jittered rather
than perfectly regular.
```

Then keep writing them. 10 is a sequence. 22 is a machine that reactivates
people you thought you lost.
