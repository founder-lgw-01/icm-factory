## What ICM is, in plain words

A business gives an AI assistant a filing cabinet instead of a personality.

Files and folders, laid out so the assistant can always find the right thing,
with a short page at the front telling it where everything lives. One
assistant. No swarm, no orchestra, no router.

Every claim underneath the whole offer is this: **the organizing is the
product, not the assistant.** People buy assistants and get nothing, because
the assistant was never the missing part.

**Never call it context management, an architecture, or a system design in
anything public.** It is files and folders. That plain name is the entire moat,
and the plain-words name is the entire positioning.

## The 2 markets

Know which one is being talked about at all times. They buy different things
for different reasons and they never get addressed with the same words.

### Market 1: the businesses drowning

Small and medium businesses that tried AI and are worse off. Scored against
the 4 Signs in `the-4-signs.md`.

They buy relief. They pay to stop feeling stupid about a thing they already
spent money on.

### Market 2: the builders

People who built a small AI site or a few assistants and want to sell AI to
businesses. They have no niche, never did the research, and are afraid of
selling. Every AI community is full of them and the count is climbing.

They buy a path. They pay to watch someone go first.

**They are also the bigger market.** The
research on market 1 is itself the product sold to market 2: they watch a real
market get validated in public, then buy the full version.

One buyer can be both. The worked example in `the-4-signs.md` is a business owner drowning
AND someone asking to learn how to do it. When that happens, sell relief first.
Nobody learns while they are underwater.

## The hole in the middle

The thesis the research has to prove or break:

> Jake serves the top. Nothing serious serves the bottom. The businesses at the
> bottom have the same problem, less money, no vocabulary, and no patience for
> procurement.

Supporting evidence already in hand, from the author's own inbound:

- The people arriving cannot speak enterprise and do not want to.
- They want to help small and medium businesses and do not know how.
- Nobody visible has positioned an AI service for that layer properly. The
  pages are generic, or the builder is buried in making the impressive thing,
  or the idea is good and they cannot sell it.

**A researcher who confirms this without evidence has failed.** Look hard for
who is already selling AI cleanup to small businesses. If a real competitor
layer exists, that is the most valuable finding the research can return, and it
is worth more than a comfortable yes.

## The author's evidence: the 35-build corpus

The author holds a number nobody else has published, and it is the proof for market
2. Source: a line-by-line audit of a public build competition. Quote it exactly; do not re-derive it.

35 real builds were read line by line and scored against 4 known ways they go
wrong.

| Ways it went wrong | How many builds |
|---|---|
| 0 of 4 | 2 |
| 1 of 4 | 12 |
| 2 of 4 | 10 |
| 3 of 4 | 10 |
| 4 of 4 | 1 |

**Typical build carried 2 of the 4. Only 2 of the 35 were clean.**

The 4 ways, in plain words:

1. **The rules are only written down, not enforced.** 24 of 35.
2. **The example contradicts the rule it is demonstrating.** 11 of 35.
3. **It was never run on anything real.** 19 of 35.
4. **The folder built to collect results is empty.** 12 of 35.

**How to use this number, and the tone is not optional.** Everybody carrying 2
is average. Saying so out loud is what lets them hear the rest. Nobody is
behind. This number exists to be specific about what to fix first, never to
grade anyone.

**Honest limits, state them whenever the number gets used:** it is one
competition, so it over-represents people building for an audience and
under-represents quiet internal work. Do not present it as the state of the
field. Present it as 35 builds read carefully, which is 35 more than anyone
else has published.

## What is already true, so nobody researches it again

- ICM works. The author runs on it, and so do the people who follow him.
- One assistant plus good files beats a swarm for this size of business.
- the author is roughly 60 days into AI and 15 years into running businesses. That
  gap is an advantage, not a hole. The buyer's own words for it: the person
  slightly ahead of you gives better advice than the expert who forgot what
  being new felt like.
- Write your own standing assets here, so nobody researches what you already
  own: your domain, your lead source, your community, your proof.

None of the above needs a researcher spent on it.

## What the research must actually answer

Everything else is background. These 5 are the job:

1. **Which businesses, specifically, are drowning?** Named segments, ranked. Not
   "small businesses."
2. **How many of them are there, and how was that counted?**
3. **What does it cost them today,** in their hours and their dollars?
4. **Who else is already selling to them, if anyone?**
5. **Will they pay $1,000 to $5,000 up front plus $300 to $700 a month?**

A report that answers 4 of these 5 is not done.
